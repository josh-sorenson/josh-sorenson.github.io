About:
NASA awarded the Utah State University Propulsion Research Laboratory a grant to develop a hybrid rocket motor that simulates lunar landing thrusters. The propellants chosen were gaseous oxygen and ABS plastic. The motor was rigorously tested and iteratively optimized for performance. The final configuration completed more than 100 tests, including 70 hot-fire tests at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center. In 2026 the motor will be installed in the NASA Langley vacuum chamber with lunar regolith simulant to collect plume-surface interaction data.

Objectives and Outcome:
I joined the program after the initial design and prototype hot-firing were complete. My contributions focused on motor performance improvements, hot-fire testing support, and test-stand enhancements. To tune the regression rate I modified the fuel-grain geometry; because the grains were 3D-printed ABS, design iterations were rapid and optimal port sizing was achieved quickly. I redesigned the tool cart and testing workflow to eliminate setup and teardown bottlenecks, cutting total testing time in half. On the test stand I eliminated leaks and added shielding and grounds to cables to reduce data noise. I also served as test conductor for numerous hot-fire tests and processed data from several tests to verify the results of other students.
