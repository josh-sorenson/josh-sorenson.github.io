**About:**
Hybird Space Systems awarded the Utah State University Propulsion Research Laboratory two grants to develop hybrid rocket motors. The first grant focused on a strap-on deorbit motor capable of deorbiting a satellite at end-of-mission life using a nitrous oxide / gaseous oxygen blend with ABS fuel; extensive testing on a work-horse motor led to an optimized injector, fuel grain geometry, and ground-test nozzle such that five perigee burns will successfully deorbit the target satellite. The second grant developed scalable motors targeting 1000 lbf thrust, producing optimized units at 100 lbf, 250 lbf, and 325 lbf scales before Hybird pivoted the effort toward nitrous oxide direct ignition. Both programs utilized the same test infrastructure and shared many development activities.

**Objectives and Outcome:**
I supported both programs through extensive hardware fabrication, testing, and operations. I machined nozzles and other components on the lathe and mill, procured stock material, and helped manufacture the scaled motors. For both motors I modified fuel grain and nozzle geometries to tune chamber pressure and regression rate. I assembled and disassembled the motors for more than 25 combined hot-fire tests and served as test conductor, running the hot-fire sequence and data acquisition for over 20 tests. I also handled the nitrous oxide blend, manufacturing it prior to each test, and participated in weekly status meetings with Hybird Space Systems. The deorbit motor test stand mounting point was modified for rapid installation and removal, while the scalable motors benefited from stock-part procurement and geometry iterations that enabled rapid thrust scaling up to 325 lbf.
