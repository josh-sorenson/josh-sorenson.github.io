--- title: "Thesis Project" excerpt: "Utah State University Propulsion Research Laboratory
" collection: portfolio --- **About:** In the field of hypersonic testing, there is a market gap between simulations and test facilities. My thesis topic seeks to fill that gap by using a low-cost hybrid rocket as a high-enthalpy gas generator to simulate hypersonic conditions. To adjust plume temperatures, nitrogen is injected into the plume at the nozzle exit. A sample test article is placed downstream of the plume at a specified distance and is fitted with temperature and pressure ports to obtain stagnation temperature, stagnation pressure, and downstream pressure at specified locations on the article. **Objectives and Outcome:** My work on this project centered on improving the technology readiness level (TRL) beyond an initial validation attempt conducted before I began my thesis. Because plume temperatures exceeded the extrapolated Mach number range of interest, I redesigned the test article mounting system and developed a nitrogen injection system to cool the plume. I conducted initial tests without a test article to refine the test process and data acquisition system, then incorporated a previously used wedge test article for validation. Recognizing that an axisymmetric test article would better suit the axisymmetric gas generator, I designed and built two blunt-body test articles and successfully tested both designs. I also machined graphite nozzles to support the testing and developed data processing code for future work with this technology in the Propulsion Research Laboratory. The work was highlighted in a paper that I presented at SciTech 2026, which is now published in the conference proceedings.
